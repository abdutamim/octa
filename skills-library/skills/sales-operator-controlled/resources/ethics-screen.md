# Sales Operator Internal Ethics Screen v0

Status: `INTERNAL CONTROLLED DEVELOPMENT — NOT PRODUCTION APPROVED`

Source basis: SKILL.md (controlled draft, HAD-003), Sales-Operator_Human-Approval-Decisions-v0.md (HAD-001/002), Cross-Book Synthesis v2 §7 (Ethics Boundary Map), Candidate Principles v0 §6 (Candidate Ethics Guardrails), Source-Gap Map v2 §5 (High-Risk Areas), and the six candidate knowledge files. Classification: Derived synthesis of the existing guardrail layers, with workflow structure as labeled Original extension. This document creates no restricted outputs and approves nothing.

## 1. Purpose

This is an **internal governance and review tool, not a selling tool**. It exists to evaluate any sales idea, tactic, message concept, strategy, diagnostic aid, or proposed development track *before* it is used in reasoning, developed further, or queued for human approval.

It does not help sell. It helps decide whether something *may be thought about, built, or escalated* — and on what terms. Its outputs are verdicts and redirections, never refined tactics. Passing this screen does not constitute production approval; it only means an idea is safe for continued controlled internal work under HAD-003.

## 2. Core Review Question

Every review starts and ends with one question:

> **"Does this idea help the buyer make a clearer, freer, better-informed decision — or does it pressure, distort, corner, or manipulate the buyer?"**

If the honest answer contains any amount of "pressure, distort, corner, or manipulate," the idea fails, regardless of how effective it might be. Effectiveness is never a defense under this governance: an idea that only works when the buyer doesn't notice it, can't evaluate it, or can't refuse it is disqualified by that fact alone.

## 3. Mandatory Pass / Fail Gates

All 12 gates must pass. One fail = the idea fails the screen and goes to the Ethical Redirection Rule (§5). "The mechanism" below means the thing that makes the idea work.

### Gate 1 — Buyer Autonomy

- **What to check:** Does the idea's mechanism depend on the buyer reasoning freely, or on the buyer's judgment being bypassed, narrowed without consent, or overridden? Would the idea still work if the buyer fully understood what was being done?
- **Pass:** The idea works *because* the buyer evaluates freely — it adds information, clarity, or choice. Full transparency would not break it.
- **Fail:** The idea works by suppressing, channeling, or substituting for the buyer's own judgment; disclosure of the mechanism would defeat it.
- **Governance source:** SKILL.md §5 (Mandatory Buyer-Autonomy Rule); HAD-002 principle 6 (buyer autonomy before conversion); Getting to Yes role (autonomy, anti-pressure).

### Gate 2 — Truthfulness

- **What to check:** Is every factual element — claims, evidence, constraints, comparisons, expertise — actually true, and represented at its true strength? Includes cherry-picking checks: true facts arranged to create a false impression fail.
- **Pass:** All cues and claims are genuine, representative, and would survive the buyer independently verifying them.
- **Fail:** Anything is invented, exaggerated, selectively framed to mislead, or stated with more certainty than the evidence supports.
- **Governance source:** HAD-002 principle 8 (truthful cues only); HAD-001 (fake reviews, fake mutual gain, fake objective criteria all blocked); Influence role (genuine vs. fabricated cue line).

### Gate 3 — No Fabricated Cues

- **What to check:** Does the idea manufacture or counterfeit any influence trigger — scarcity, urgency, authority, social proof, reciprocity, liking/similarity, unity, or criteria — rather than disclosing a real one?
- **Pass:** Any influence cue present is real, material to the buyer's decision, and disclosed as fact rather than deployed as trigger.
- **Fail:** Any cue is manufactured, staged, borrowed, inflated, or timed/designed primarily to trigger shortcut compliance.
- **Governance source:** HAD-001 (fabricated influence cues and all fake-cue variants blocked); Influence role (fabricated-cue detection); Cross-Book Synthesis v2 §7.

### Gate 4 — No Pressure

- **What to check:** Does the idea use force-of-will mechanisms — deadlines as leverage, tension directed at the person, repeated asks, silence as discomfort, commitment momentum, take-it-or-leave-it framing, authority as weight? Test: does it yield to principle or to pressure (the GTY standard)?
- **Pass:** The buyer can slow down, push back, or decline at every point without cost engineered by the seller; disagreement is resolved by standards and merits.
- **Fail:** The idea makes refusal, delay, or deliberation costly, uncomfortable, or socially awkward by design.
- **Governance source:** HAD-001 (threats, strategic silence, anchoring-as-tactic, commitment traps blocked); SPIN role (closing-pressure findings); Getting to Yes role (anti-pressure stance).

### Gate 5 — No Fear Exploitation

- **What to check:** Does the idea amplify fear, anxiety, or perceived risk beyond what buyer facts support? Distinguish *clarifying real impact* (legitimate, Gap/SPIN) from *manufacturing or escalating fear to drive action*. Special check: is the target buyer already anxious or indecisive? (JOLT: pressure on a fearful buyer correlates with worse outcomes — fear amplification fails on both ethics and evidence.)
- **Pass:** Impact and risk are developed strictly from genuine buyer facts; when fear or indecision appears, the idea shifts toward risk reduction, not escalation.
- **Fail:** The idea inflates pain, invents consequences, relitigates resolved fears, or uses dread to compress deliberation.
- **Governance source:** HAD-002 principle 7 (risk reduction without urgency manipulation); JOLT role (decision fear); Cross-Book Synthesis v2 §5 (pain expansion vs. manipulation risk tension).

### Gate 6 — No Overload Exploitation

- **What to check:** Does the idea exploit complexity, information volume, option flooding, or time compression to push the buyer onto shortcut cues or into dependence on the seller?
- **Pass:** The idea simplifies honestly — it reduces the buyer's cognitive burden while keeping real options and tradeoffs visible.
- **Fail:** Complexity, jargon, volume, or pace is used (or tolerated) because the resulting confusion benefits the seller.
- **Governance source:** HAD-001 (overload exploitation blocked); Influence role (Chapter 9 overload thesis); JOLT role (option overload and indecision).

### Gate 7 — No Identity / Unity Manipulation

- **What to check:** Does the idea manufacture belonging, shared identity, "we" framing, affinity, or in-group dynamics so refusal feels like disloyalty or betrayal?
- **Pass:** Any shared identity referenced is genuine, incidental, and carries no engineered cost of refusal.
- **Fail:** Belonging is manufactured, similarity is faked, or group identity is leveraged so saying no has social or identity cost.
- **Governance source:** HAD-001 (unity/identity manipulation blocked); Influence role (Unity flagged very-high-risk, buyer-protection concept only).

### Gate 8 — No Buyer-BATNA Weaponization

- **What to check:** Does the idea touch the buyer's alternatives? BATNA reasoning is self-protective only — judging one's own walk-away point. Check for: disparaging or distorting the buyer's alternatives, working to worsen them, or using the buyer's weak alternatives as leverage.
- **Pass:** BATNA reasoning stays internal/self-protective; the buyer's alternatives are treated as legitimate, and an honestly better buyer alternative is acknowledged as such.
- **Fail:** The idea targets, degrades, misrepresents, or exploits the buyer's no-deal option to corner them into agreement.
- **Governance source:** HAD-001 (buyer-BATNA weaponization blocked); HAD-002 principle 10 (BATNA as self-protection only); Getting to Yes role (BATNA boundaries).

### Gate 9 — No Dirty Tricks as Tactics

- **What to check:** Does the idea resemble anything in the dirty-tricks catalog — phony facts, ambiguous authority, good-guy/bad-guy, extreme or escalating demands, lock-ins, calculated delay, stressful settings, personal attacks? Also check for laundering: detection knowledge repackaged as "what works."
- **Pass:** No catalog resemblance; any dirty-trick knowledge present is used solely to recognize and resist.
- **Fail:** Any cataloged tactic appears as a recommended or implied seller behavior, however renamed or softened.
- **Governance source:** HAD-001 (dirty tricks as tactics blocked; detection defensive-only); Getting to Yes role (dirty-trick detection).

### Gate 10 — No-Deal Remains Legitimate

- **What to check:** Does the idea preserve "no deal" as a genuine outcome? Check the idea's implicit objective: does it assume conversion is the only success state? Would the idea ever recommend the buyer walk away when their interests say so?
- **Pass:** The idea has an honest exit path — it can conclude in "this isn't a fit" and treats that as a valid result, not a failure to be overcome.
- **Fail:** The idea is structurally conversion-locked: every buyer state and objection routes back toward yes.
- **Governance source:** HAD-002 principle 9 (no-deal legitimacy); SKILL.md §5; Getting to Yes role (no-agreement as acceptable outcome).

### Gate 11 — Objective Criteria Are Real and Contestable

- **What to check:** Where the idea invokes standards, benchmarks, comparisons, or "fairness": are the criteria genuine, independent of the seller's will, relevant to this buyer, and open to challenge? Would the seller accept the same criteria if they cut the other way (the reciprocity test)? Can the buyer propose competing standards?
- **Pass:** Criteria are independently verifiable, reciprocally applicable, and the idea invites the buyer to test or counter them.
- **Fail:** Criteria are fabricated, cherry-picked, seller-controlled, immune to challenge, or function as legitimacy theater for a predetermined conclusion.
- **Governance source:** HAD-001 (fake objective criteria, consistency/legitimacy engineering blocked); HAD-002 principle 5 (objective criteria before pressure); Getting to Yes role (objective criteria, fair process).

### Gate 12 — Statistics and Claims Are Verified or Clearly Qualified

- **What to check:** Every number, study, or research finding in the idea against the candidate files' evidence status: verified-in-source, sharpened/rounded, figure-only, proprietary-methodology (Tethr/CEB/Gong), correlational, body-text-only, anecdotal, or rejected/fabricated. Check that correlations aren't stated as causes, relative lifts carry base rates, and anecdotes aren't used as proof.
- **Pass:** Every claim is either verified at its source with caveats intact, or explicitly qualified (e.g., "proprietary single-source dataset, correlational"), or removed.
- **Fail:** Any unverified, rejected, sharpened, or proprietary figure presented as settled fact; any anecdote as evidence; any causal language on correlational findings.
- **Governance source:** HAD-001 (exact unverified statistics as claims; historical anecdotes as proof — both blocked); SKILL.md §9 (Evidence and Claims Rules); Source-Gap Map v2 §4.

## 4. Defensive-Only Detection Layer

Status of this entire section: `DEFENSIVE-ONLY — NOT FOR OFFENSIVE USE — NOT IMPLEMENTATION APPROVED` (per HAD-001 §3). These entries exist so manipulation can be **detected, resisted, and removed** — in others' behavior aimed at you, or in your own side's drafts and plans before they ship. Conceptual detection signals only; no scripts.

| Pattern | Detection signal | Why it is risky | Allowed use | Forbidden use |
| ------- | ---------------- | --------------- | ----------- | ------------- |
| Fake scarcity | A claimed limit (stock, seats, capacity) that can't be independently confirmed, conveniently resets, or appears only at decision moments | Compresses deliberation through loss aversion; exploits anxious buyers most | Detection / resistance / removal only | Creating, suggesting, or implying artificial limits |
| Fake urgency | A deadline with no verifiable external cause; expiring terms where the consequence is seller-imposed will, not independent fact | Converts time itself into pressure; JOLT-layer evidence suggests it worsens outcomes for fearful buyers | Detection / resistance / removal only | Manufacturing deadlines or "fading opportunity" framing |
| Fake authority | Credentials irrelevant to the claim; borrowed or unverifiable titles; authority symbols substituting for checkable evidence; "trust me" in place of reasons | Triggers deference that suppresses independent evaluation | Detection / resistance / removal only | Deploying status, titles, or expert framing as compliance levers |
| Fake social proof | Popularity claims without verifiable basis; testimonials that are unrepresentative, incentivized, or curated to hide failures; adoption numbers that don't reconcile | Substitutes herd inference for fit analysis; manufactured consensus is self-reinforcing | Detection / resistance / removal only | Staging, inflating, or cherry-picking consensus evidence |
| Fake reviews | Review patterns inconsistent with organic use (timing clusters, language uniformity); unverifiable reviewers; suppressed negatives | Directly fabricated evidence — fails truthfulness at the root | Detection / resistance / removal only | Creating, soliciting-for-bias, or laundering fabricated review content |
| Fake objective criteria | Benchmarks the proposer controls; standards that always favor one side; refusal to apply criteria reciprocally; "industry standard" with no checkable source | The most camouflaged pattern — pressure disguised as fairness ("legitimacy engineering") | Detection / resistance / removal only | Constructing or curating pseudo-standards to steer a conclusion |
| Manipulation pressure | Deliberation made costly; refusal made awkward; pace forced; emotional discomfort engineered (silence, repetition, escalating asks, commitment momentum) | Substitutes will-contest for merits; corrodes consent quality | Detection / resistance / removal only | Applying any pressure mechanism, including "soft" variants |
| Dirty tricks | Recognizable catalog patterns: phony facts, ambiguous authority, good-guy/bad-guy, extreme openings, escalating demands, lock-ins, stressful settings, calculated delay, personal attacks | Each defeats fair process; collectively they define bad-faith negotiation | Detection / resistance / removal only — including naming the tactic and negotiating the rules of the game | Using, adapting, or renaming any catalog item as a tactic |
| Buyer-BATNA weaponization | The buyer's alternatives being disparaged, distorted, or degraded; "you have no real choice" framing; leverage built from the buyer's weak position | Converts the buyer's vulnerability into seller power — coercion in negotiation form | Detection / resistance / removal only; BATNA reasoning stays self-protective | Targeting, worsening, or exploiting the buyer's no-deal option |

**Laundering rule** *(Original extension)*: any request to "improve," "soften," or "make defensible" a detected pattern is treated as an attempt to convert detection into tactic, and fails the screen automatically. The only valid outputs from detection are: name it, resist it, remove it, or redirect it (§5).

## 5. Ethical Redirection Rule

A failed idea is not discarded — it is usually a legitimate need expressed through an illegitimate mechanism. Redirection means: **identify the legitimate need underneath, then route it through an approved channel.** (Conceptual redirection only; no rewritten customer-facing material.)

| When the failed idea was trying to... | Redirect into... |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Create urgency or momentum (fake scarcity/urgency, deadlines, momentum tactics) | **Risk reduction without urgency** — if the buyer isn't moving, diagnose *why* (JOLT: fear? unclear impact?); address the real blocker. A real constraint may be disclosed as verifiable fact, never deployed as a trigger. |
| Convince through borrowed weight (fake authority, social proof, credibility theater) | **Diagnosis + objective criteria** — replace "believe me/them" with checkable evidence, real and relevant expertise, and standards the buyer can contest. |
| Make the buyer feel the problem more (pain amplification, fear inflation) | **Buyer-owned discovery** — develop real impact from the buyer's own facts and words (SPIN implication logic, Gap impact/root-cause), and stop where the facts stop. |
| Overcome hesitation or objections (pressure, commitment traps, overload) | **Decision-risk diagnosis + risk reduction** — distinguish "doesn't see value" from "fears the decision"; reduce real risk, clarify expectations, simplify honestly. |
| Steer the buyer to the preferred option (hidden narrowing, decoys, anchoring) | **Clearer options without removing agency** — transparent comparison on real tradeoffs and genuine criteria; narrowing only openly, with consent, with alternatives visible (JOLT narrowing bounded by GTY agency). |
| Win a deal that may not fit (BATNA weaponization, conversion-locked logic) | **No-deal / fit assessment** — run the honest fit question; if the buyer's alternative is genuinely better, the ethical output is acknowledging it. A no-deal verdict reached honestly protects long-term trust. |

If no legitimate need can be found underneath the idea — the idea *was* the manipulation — it is not redirected; it is blocked and logged.

## 6. Output Decision Categories

Every reviewed idea lands in exactly one category (lowest applicable category wins *(Original extension)*; e.g., an idea that is both unverified and pressure-based is **Blocked**, not **Needs verification**):

1. **Allowed for internal reasoning** — passes all 12 gates; falls within the allowed output types (internal reasoning, analysis, checklists, draft conceptual frameworks). May be developed further internally. Not production approval.
2. **Needs explicit approval** — passes the ethics gates but its natural output form is a restricted type (module, lesson, script, customer-facing example, final framework, industry adaptation), or it touches a gated concept (Challenger-style tension, persuasion cues, recommendation narrowing, BATNA-related content, risk-reduction tactics). Parked until the user approves that specific output or use.
3. **Defensive-only** — valid only as detection/resistance/protection knowledge (§4 layer). May be developed for buyer-protection and self-audit purposes; permanently barred from offensive use absent a new HAD-level decision.
4. **Blocked** — depends on a HAD-001 blocked concept, fails an ethics gate at its mechanism (not just its wording), or is a laundering attempt. Not developed, not refined, not "fixed." Logged, with redirection offered where a legitimate underlying need exists.
5. **Needs verification** — ethically sound but resting on claims with unresolved evidence status (Tethr/CEB/Gong proprietary stats, body-text figures, sharpened numbers). Goes to the verification queue; may proceed only with claims removed or explicitly qualified.
6. **Needs source coverage** — ethically sound but outside what the six sources can support (per Source-Gap Map v2: listening science, buying committees, post-sale, coaching, measurement, legal/compliance). May be developed only as a clearly-labeled Original extension, or parked until a covering source is ingested and approved.

## 7. Review Workflow

1. **Identify the idea or proposed action.** State it plainly, including what makes it work (the mechanism), who it targets, and in what buyer state.
2. **Classify output type.** What would this become — internal reasoning? checklist? something script-shaped or customer-facing?
3. **Check whether it is restricted.** If the output type is on the restricted list, stop and route to explicit approval before any further development.
4. **Run the 12 pass/fail gates** (§3). Record any fail and which gate. One fail → §5 redirection.
5. **Check evidence status.** Tag every factual claim against the candidate files' verification tables; flag anything unverified, proprietary, correlational, or rejected.
6. **Check buyer autonomy last as a holistic re-test.** Re-ask the Core Review Question (§2) about the idea *as a whole* — gates can pass individually while the combination still corners the buyer.
7. **Decide category** (§6) and label the idea's classification (Source-backed / Derived synthesis / Original extension).
8. **If safe, keep it internal** — marked `INTERNAL CONTROLLED DEVELOPMENT — NOT PRODUCTION APPROVED` — unless and until future explicit approval allows implementation. Log boundary-touching decisions in the decisions log.
