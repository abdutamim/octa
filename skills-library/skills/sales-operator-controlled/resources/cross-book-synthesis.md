## 3. Shared Themes Across the Six Sources

- Buyer-owned discovery: SPIN and Gap foreground buyer-specific needs, problems, facts, impact, and explicit value.
- Problem and impact development: SPIN's implication logic and Gap's current-state / future-state gap both develop consequence before solution.
- Teaching and reframing: Challenger adds outside-in insight, but its own candidate layer keeps tension and control unapproved and boundary-sensitive.
- Risk reduction: JOLT and Getting to Yes both protect against bad decisions, though JOLT focuses on buyer indecision and GTY focuses on BATNA, objective criteria, and fair process.
- Fair agreement: Getting to Yes adds the strongest source language around mutual gain, objective standards, fair procedures, and no agreement when the offer is worse than BATNA.
- Buyer autonomy: All six sources warn, directly or indirectly, against pressure, false certainty, manufactured cues, scripts, or coercive commitments.
- Objective criteria: Getting to Yes provides the clearest candidate guardrail for moving price, terms, and conflict away from will or pressure.
- Pressure resistance: SPIN warns against closing pressure; JOLT warns against urgency with anxious buyers; Influence and GTY flag pressure mechanisms directly.
- Ethical persuasion: Influence distinguishes genuine cues from fabricated cues; GTY distinguishes principle from pressure and dirty tricks from legitimate negotiation.
- Fabricated cue detection: Influence maps fake social proof, fake authority, fake scarcity, fake liking, fake reciprocity, fake unity, and overload exploitation; GTY adds fake criteria, fake warnings, threats, and dirty tricks.
- No-deal as legitimate when appropriate: Getting to Yes adds the explicit candidate principle that no agreement can be ethical when no deal is better than the proposed agreement.

## 8. Candidate Principles for Later Review

- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Diagnosis before persuasion: buyer-specific facts, problems, interests, and risks should be understood before influence cues are used.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Interests before positions: stated positions may need interest-mapping, but interests must not be used to override boundaries.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Objective criteria before pressure: price, terms, and contested issues should be grounded in genuine criteria where possible.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Buyer autonomy before conversion: a sale is not ethical if it requires pressure, deception, fabricated cues, or ignoring a better no-deal option.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Risk reduction without urgency manipulation: anxious buyers should not be pushed with scarcity, fear, or artificial deadlines.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. No deal remains legitimate when the offer is not in both parties' interests or is worse than a party's BATNA.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Truthful cues only: authority, social proof, scarcity, liking, reciprocity, and criteria must be genuine.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Fabricated cues are prohibited.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Dirty tricks are detection-only and resistance-only, never tactics.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. BATNA is self-protection only, never buyer pressure.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Challenger teaching must remain bounded by buyer validation, objective criteria, and no-pressure guardrails.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Option generation must be balanced against JOLT-style indecision and overload risk.
- CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Commitment steps are unsafe unless voluntary, transparent, and buyer-owned.
