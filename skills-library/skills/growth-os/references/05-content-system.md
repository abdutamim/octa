# 05 — Content System

## The 3C funnel

Every single post must be assigned to exactly one stage. A post with no stage
is a post with no purpose.

| Stage | Job | Formats | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Connect** | Be seen by people who don't know you | trend-based reel, story, hot take, relatable moment | Reach, % non-followers |
| **Consider** | Earn trust and be worth following | how-to, framework, teardown, behind-the-scenes | Saves, shares, profile visits |
| **Close** | Convert to a customer | proof, case study, offer, "who this is not for" | DMs, link clicks, sales |

**Ratio: 3:1 value to sell.** Three Connect/Consider posts for every Close post.
Breaking this ratio is the most common cause of an audience going cold.

---

## Content pillars (3–5, no more)

A pillar is only valid if it passes the test:
**can you list 20 post ideas for it right now, without research?**
If not, it is a topic, not a pillar.

Typical pillar set for a personal brand:
```
1. Teach   — the thing you're expert at
2. Prove   — results, cases, receipts
3. Opinion — what you think the field gets wrong
4. Story   — your journey, failures, lessons
5. Human   — life, taste, personality
```

---

## Depth levels: L1 / L2 / L3

| Level | What it is | Give it away? |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | What to do | Yes — free, publicly |
| L2 | Why it works | Yes — free, this is what builds authority |
| L3 | Exactly how, with your assets, on your case | No — this is the paid product |

Giving away L1 and L2 generously is what makes L3 sellable. Hoarding L2 is the
most common self-sabotage in expert content.

## Series structure

- **Credibility series** — recurring proof format (weekly result, teardown).
- **Reach series** — the repeatable format built for new eyes.
- **Conversion series** — recurring offer or objection content.
- **Anchor post every 2 weeks** — your single highest-effort piece. Everything
  else can be lighter if the anchor lands.

**Repeatable formats are the single strongest structural bet right now.** A named,
recurring series or challenge ( "Should I open it or keep it sealed?", "I run a
mile for every follower") beats random posting because it is easy for you to
make and easy for the audience to recognize and return to.

---

## 5 idea sources

1. **Customer questions** — DMs, comments, sales calls. Highest hit rate.
2. **Your own past confusion** — what you misunderstood 2 years ago.
3. **Competitor comment sections** — complaints are content briefs.
4. **Adjacent-niche formats** — a format that works in fitness will work in
   finance; the format is not owned by the niche.
5. **Your own winners** — see below.

---

## 1 winner = 5 posts

When a post performs, do not move on. Reissue it as:
1. Same idea, different format (reel → carousel).
2. Same idea, opposite angle ("why this is wrong").
3. Deeper dive on one sub-point.
4. Case study applying it.
5. Q&A answering the top comment.

Most accounts underexploit their winners by an order of magnitude.

---

## Visual diet

Define what your grid looks like as a whole: which formats appear, in what
proportion, with what recurring visual signature (a fixed color band, a fixed
type treatment, a fixed opening frame). Recognition beats novelty.

---

## 5 story angles

```
1. The beginning  — where you started, honestly
2. The failure    — what broke and what it cost
3. The turn       — what changed and why
4. The lesson     — the transferable principle
5. Where I am now — current reality, no exaggeration
```

---

## Kill-off filter (8 questions — fewer than 6 yes = discard)

```
1. Is it for one specific person?
2. Does it match their awareness stage?
3. Can I write the literal hook in under 6 words?
4. Does it teach, prove, or provoke — not just describe?
5. Can I produce it with what I have today?
6. Does it belong to a pillar?
7. Does it have a clear stage and CTA?
8. Would I stop scrolling for it myself?
```
