# 07 — Copywriting

## The 3 rules (Harry Dry)

1. **Be specific.** "Fast delivery" → "At your door in 45 minutes."
2. **Be visual.** Write words that produce a picture. If the reader cannot see
   it, they will not remember it.
3. **Be falsifiable.** If a competitor could copy-paste your sentence, delete it.

### 3 techniques
- **Rhyme, rhythm, alliteration** — memorable beats clever.
- **Contrast** — "Not X. Y." The shape itself carries meaning.
- **Show the mechanism** — "1,000 songs in your pocket" beats "5GB storage".

## 9 formulas

| Formula | Structure | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **AIDA** | Attention → Interest → Desire → Action | ads, general |
| **PAS** | Problem → Agitate → Solution | pain-driven markets |
| **BAB** | Before → After → Bridge | transformations |
| **4Ps** | Picture → Promise → Prove → Push | landing pages |
| **FAB** | Features → Advantages → Benefits | product pages |
| **PASTOR** | Problem, Amplify, Story, Testimony, Offer, Response | long sales pages |
| **4 U's** | Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific | headlines |
| **QUEST** | Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition | webinars, emails |
| **Star-Story-Solution** | hero → journey → product | personal brand |

## Caption structure

```
Line 1   The hook, repeated or sharpened. Must survive the "...more" cut.
Line 2   White space.
Body     Short lines. One idea per line. No walls of text.
Proof    A number, a name, a screenshot reference.
CTA      One action.
```
The caption should **add** to the video, not transcribe it.

## Landing page — 10 sections

```
1.  Headline        the promise, specific and falsifiable
2.  Subheadline     who it's for + the mechanism
3.  Hero proof      one number, logo row, or result
4.  Problem         in their own words (from the VOC bank)
5.  Mechanism       why this works, named
6.  What you get    the stack, each line deliverable
7.  Proof           testimonials, cases, screenshots
8.  Offer & price   3 tiers, guarantee in plain language
9.  FAQ             the real objections, answered directly
10. Final CTA       same action as the top, repeated
```

**Match the first screen to the ad, literally.** If the ad said one thing and the
page says another, the click is wasted no matter how good the page is.

## UX psychology that moves conversion

- **Smart defaults** — preselect the option most people should choose.
- **Endowed progress** — show step 1 already complete.
- **Fewer choices** — more options means more deferral, not more sales.
- **Social proof next to the button**, not in a distant section.
- **Total cost visible early** — surprise shipping is the top cart-abandon cause.
- **One primary action per screen.** Everything else is secondary styling.
- Mobile first. Thumb reach. Tap targets ≥ 44px.

## Email / WhatsApp sequences

### Welcome (5 emails, days 1–5)
```
1. Deliver what was promised + set expectations
2. Your story / why you do this
3. Your point of view — what the field gets wrong
4. A concrete win they can get today
5. Soft introduction of the offer
```

### FOMO series (4 emails, days 6–9)
```
6. Detailed testimonial or case study
7. The impact — what changes for people who take this
8. Objection handling, directly
9. Clear next step: call, offer doc, or checkout link
```

By day 9 someone who joined your list has been sold to properly — without a
single manual sales call.

## Anti-slop — banned phrases

```
✗ In today's fast-paced world
✗ Unlock the power of
✗ Take your X to the next level
✗ Game-changer / revolutionary / cutting-edge
✗ It's not just X, it's Y  (unless earned)
✗ Let's dive in / dive deep
✗ Elevate · seamless · robust · leverage · synergy
✗ Rhetorical question stacks with no answers
✗ Em-dash-heavy triads that say nothing
```

Also banned: adjectives with no number behind them, promises with no mechanism,
and any sentence that would be equally true for a competitor.

---

## Final copy checklist

```
☐ Would a real person say this out loud?
☐ Is there a number, name, or image in the first two lines?
☐ Could a competitor publish this unchanged? (if yes, rewrite)
☐ One idea, one CTA
☐ Written in the audience's actual vocabulary
☐ Every claim is either provable or explicitly framed as belief
☐ Read it aloud — anywhere you stumble, the reader stumbles
```
