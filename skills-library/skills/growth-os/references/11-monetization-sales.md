# 11 — Monetization & Sales

## Monetization paths

| Path | When it fits | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Service | Fastest to revenue | Needs proof and a fast DM loop |
| Digital product | After repeat demand appears | Build it from audience questions |
| Sponsorships | Steady reach + defined audience | Niche matters more than size |
| Affiliate | Recommendation-style content | Credibility costs more than the commission |
| Physical product | Demand already proven | Operations is the real risk |

**Sequencing rule:** service → productized service → digital product. Reverse
order fails because you build before you know what people will pay for.

---

## Productized service

Replace "I do marketing" with a named, fixed package:

```
Name · Promise · What's in and what's out · Duration · Price · Deliverables · Guarantee
```

This raises price, cuts negotiation, and makes the result repeatable.

**Small paid entry point** — an audit, teardown, or diagnostic session. Far easier
to sell than the flagship, and buyers of it convert to the flagship at a much
higher rate than cold leads.

## How buying actually works

```
People buy with emotion and justify with logic.
Emotion makes them say yes. Logic makes them not regret it.
```
Supply both. Emotion in the hook and the story; logic in the mechanism, the
numbers, and the guarantee.

## 7 sales rules

1. **Ask more than you talk.** A sales call is 70% questions.
2. **Do not fish for compliments.** Ask for the truth: "What's stopping you from
   starting today?"
3. **"No" is part of the job.** The worst outcome is "let me think about it".
4. **Never apologize for your price.** State it clearly, then stop talking.
5. **"Too expensive" usually means "not convinced".** Strengthen proof before
   cutting price.
6. **Cut scope, not price.**
7. **Follow up.** Three respectful follow-ups over two weeks close more than the
   first pitch does.

### DM template that gets replies
```
[A real, specific observation about their work — proves you actually looked]
[One useful note they benefit from even if they never reply — free value]
[One simple open question — NOT a request for a call in the first message]
```

## Selling without calls (the mature state)

When content has done enough trust-building, buyers arrive **pre-sold**: they
read the offer doc and purchase a high-ticket item without ever speaking to you.
This is the goal state, and it is produced by:
- consistent proof content,
- a written offer doc that answers every objection,
- an email sequence that sells while you sleep (`07`).

---

## Speaking & presence

Three levels: beginner (rushed, tense) → competent (clear) → natural (relaxed,
convincing).
- **Pause instead of filler.** Silence reads as authority.
- **Drop your intonation** at the end of a sentence. Rising pitch reads as asking
  for approval.
- Open, still posture. Hands clarify, they don't flail.
- Speak in concrete examples — the same rule as copy.

---

## Retention (the cheapest growth)

- **Immediately after purchase:** confirmation + clear expectations.
- **After delivery:** ask for feedback + send a usage guide.
- **Repurchase cycle:** define how many days until they need it again, and reach
  out slightly before that.
- **Segment the list:** new / repeat / dormant — different message for each.

---

## Ethical red line

Never build selling on exaggerated fear, impossible promises, fake scarcity, or
invented testimonials. It works once, then it destroys the brand permanently.
