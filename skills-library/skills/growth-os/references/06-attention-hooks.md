# 06 — Attention, Hooks & Scripts

## Attention is a resource, and you are competing for it

> "A wealth of information creates a poverty of attention." — Herbert Simon, 1971

Average on-screen attention span has fallen from roughly 2.5 minutes (2004) to
about 47 seconds today. Every time a post goes out it competes with the viewer's
group chat, their ex's story, and whatever the algorithm just served them.

The brain runs a millisecond cost-benefit check:
**"Is this better than everything else I could be looking at right now?"**

Consequences:
- You can spend 40 hours on a video; if the first 2 seconds lose, none of it is seen.
- The algorithm cannot push what nobody watched long enough to classify.
- **If you have no views, you do not have a content problem. You have an
  attention problem.** They are different problems with different fixes.

Production quality does not win this battle. Familiarity and curiosity do.

---

## Every piece of content has 3 layers

| Layer | What it is | How much it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Topic** | What the video is about | Least important |
| **Format** | The structure: reaction, challenge, day-in-life, before/after, street interview, tutorial, POV | **Most important** |
| **Hook** | The first 2–3 seconds | Decides whether the other two are ever seen |

**Work in the order Format → Hook → Topic.** Most people work in the reverse
order and wonder why nothing lands.

**Nobody owns a format.** Formats are free to use, and the algorithm already
knows how to distribute them. Building a structure from scratch is expensive and
low-probability. Copy the structure, insert the brand.

---

## Reverse-engineer a viral video in 15 minutes

```
1. Search the EMOTIONAL topic your audience lives in, not your product.
   Fitness supplement → "gym culture", "body transformation"
   Dentist          → things people are embarrassed to ask
   Cleaning service → "satisfying clean", "disgusting mess reveal"

2. Find 3–5 videos in that space with 1M+ views
   — FROM SMALL ACCOUNTS. A 10M-follower account's views come from its
     audience, not the format. A small account punching above its weight
     proves the FORMAT is doing the work.

3. Study 1–2 of them frame by frame:
   How does it open? First thing seen and heard? Cut speed? Energy? Pacing?
   Write the skeleton down as a blueprint.

4. Rebuild that skeleton with the brand inside it.
   Same pacing, same beats, same cuts, same transitions. Only the content changes.

5. Write a hook that wins the first 2 seconds.
```

---

## The hook must do at least one of three things

```
1. Ask a question the viewer cannot leave without answering
2. Show something visually unexpected
3. Drop a credential that makes the source instantly trusted
```
**Two of the three in one hook = near-guaranteed attention win.**

Example:
- `Pad Thai recipe` — that's a topic, not a hook.
- `Thai Express Pad Thai vs. a real Thai chef's Pad Thai` — credential +
  comparison + implied question, same information, completely different result.

**A hook must never lead with your product.** The moment the brain categorizes
the video as an ad, the decision is already made.

---

## 12 hook types

| Type | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Contradiction | "Everything you know about X is wrong" |
| Number | "3 mistakes costing you Y" |
| Question | "Why does X keep happening to you?" |
| Story open | "Last week a client told me..." |
| Callout | "If you're a [role] doing X, stop" |
| Confession | "I lost [amount] learning this" |
| Comparison | "[Cheap version] vs [expensive version]" |
| Warning | "Don't do X before you check Y" |
| Result | "This took [outcome] in [timeframe]" |
| Curiosity gap | "Nobody talks about what happens after X" |
| Visual shock | open on the unexpected image, say nothing |
| Credential | "I've done X 400 times. Here's the pattern." |

### 5 psychological mechanisms underneath them
```
Curiosity gap · Loss aversion · Social proof · Pattern interrupt · Self-identification
```

---

## Hook craft rules

- **≤ 6 words** where possible.
- **Drunk-grandma test:** if a distracted person could not understand it
  instantly, rewrite it. Simple language beats clever language every time.
- **80% of your planning time goes into the first 3 seconds.** Not the body.
- **The hook is not only words.** Three channels, all required:
  - **Verbal** — what is said
  - **Text** — what is on screen
  - **Visual + audio** — the most neglected and the most powerful. A prop, a
    face with visible eyes, an unusual location, motion, a distinct sound.
    Instagram has always been a *visual* platform; the visual hook outperforms
    the written hook.
- **Write 10 hooks, pick 1.** Never publish the first one.

### Test each candidate hook
1. Does it stop the scroll in the first second?
2. Is it about the viewer, not about me?
3. Can it be understood with sound off?
4. Does it create a gap the body of the video actually closes?

### Hook swap (advanced)
Open with a proven curiosity-driven moment, then transition seamlessly into your
actual content. This is legitimate when the dramatic moment **actually happens**
in the video. It becomes bait-and-switch — and gets punished — when the promise
is never paid off.

---

## Script structure (short form)

```
0–3s    HOOK        one of the 12 types, ≤ 6 words, visual + verbal
3–10s   CONTEXT     why this matters to them, right now
10–xx   VALUE       the actual substance, one idea only
last 3s CTA         one action, matched to the awareness stage
```

- **Micro-hook every 8–12 seconds** — a new visual, a cut, a re-open, a question.
- **Banned openings:** "Hi guys, welcome back", "In today's video", any
  self-introduction before value.
- **One idea per video.** Two ideas means neither lands.

---

## CTA ceiling

| Funnel position | Allowed CTA |
|---|---|
| TOF / Connect | Follow · save · share |
| MOF / Consider | Comment a keyword · grab the free thing · join the list |
| BOF / Close | DM · book · buy |

**One CTA per post.** Two CTAs equal zero CTAs.

## Filming & delivery

```
Quality = Clarity + Emotion + Simplicity
```
- Back camera at 1x. Audio matters more than video.
- Window light, subject facing it. Clean fixed background.
- **Go outside.** Filming in a park or on the street measurably lifts engagement
  — more variability, more dynamism, more instinctive interest.
- **Commit to the bit.** Low-effort content looks identical to everyone else's
  low-effort content. The commitment is the differentiator, not the equipment.
- Delivery: pause instead of filler sounds. Drop intonation at the end of a
  sentence — rising intonation reads as asking permission.

### Faceless path
Screen recordings · hands-only · B-roll + voiceover · text-on-video · animated
data · product macro. Same hook rules apply; the visual hook matters even more.

## Volume

**The more you post, the more you grow.** This is a direct, observed correlation,
not a motivational slogan. One documented case: 2.5 posts/day produced 20k
followers in six months; scaling to 4 posts/day produced 60k in the next six,
with *fewer* external advantages.

This works because of platform "TikTokification": distribution is interest-based,
not follower-based, so **your worst work is simply not shown**. There is no
penalty for volume — only unshown posts. The constraint is your capacity, which
is why batch production (see `09`) is the real lever.

---

## Accept the hit rate

Roughly **20% of posts generate 80% of results.** Nobody goes viral every time.
The non-viral 80% still: builds the library, trains instinct, produces hook and
format data, and keeps you present.

The fatal mistake is posting once, getting 2,000 views, panicking, and changing
everything. That 2,000-view post is data. Change one variable at a time.

## Required output per video

```markdown
Hook (literal, ≤ 6 words) · Visual hook (what is physically on screen at 0s)
Format (named, and what it was reverse-engineered from)
Full script · On-screen text · B-roll list · Caption · One CTA · Stage · KPI
```
