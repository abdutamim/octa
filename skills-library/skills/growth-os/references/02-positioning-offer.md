# 02 — Positioning & Offer

## The focus sentence (mandatory, exact form)

```
I help [who] who feel [tension] by [mechanism] — and I do not help [who not].
```

The last clause is the one that does the work. A positioning statement without
an exclusion is not positioning — it is a description.

**Test it:** if a competitor could say the same sentence, it has failed.

---

## Niche selection — 6 criteria, score /30

Score each 1–5. **Below 22/30, pick a different niche.**

```
1. Do they have money and permission to spend it?
2. Is the pain urgent, or merely annoying?
3. Can you reach them cheaply and repeatably?
4. Do you have real credibility or genuine interest here?
5. Is there room — or does one player own the entire conversation?
6. Can you produce content about this for 12 months without hating it?
```

**Specialize before you expand.** Narrow wins first, every time. You widen from a
position of strength, never from a position of invisibility.

---

## Differentiation

Three questions, and you need a real answer to at least one:
1. What do you do differently in **method**?
2. Who do you serve that others **refuse** to serve?
3. What do you **believe** that the rest of the field does not?

**Name the enemy.** Every strong brand argues against something: an idea, a
practice, a default assumption. Not a competitor by name — an idea.

---

## Harry Dry's 3 tests

Apply to every positioning line, headline, and tagline:

```
1. SPECIFIC     "fast delivery" → "at your door in 45 minutes"
2. VISUAL       can the reader see it in their head?
3. FALSIFIABLE  could a competitor copy-paste this? then delete it.
```

---

## Offer architecture

```
Promise + Mechanism + Proof + Guarantee + Bonuses
```

| Component | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Promise** | What specific outcome, by when? |
| **Mechanism** | Why does this work when other things didn't? |
| **Proof** | Why should I believe you? |
| **Guarantee** | What happens if it doesn't work? |
| **Bonuses** | What removes my remaining objections? |

**Every bonus should kill a specific objection.** A bonus that doesn't map to an
objection is noise that dilutes the core promise.

---

## Value stack

List every deliverable with its standalone value, sum it, then show the price.
The gap must be defensible — inflated stack values destroy trust faster than a
high price does.

**Scarcity only if real.** Real: limited seats, a real deadline, real inventory.
Fake countdown timers are a red line (`13`).

---

## Pricing

| Model | When |
|---|---|
| Cost-plus | Physical products with clear COGS |
| Value-based | Services with measurable outcomes — usually correct |
| Competitive | Commodity markets |
| Tiered | Almost always — 3 tiers, anchor high |

**3 tiers.** The middle one is what you want sold; the top tier exists to make it
look reasonable; the bottom tier is the entry point.

**"Too expensive" almost always means "not convinced".** Strengthen proof and
mechanism before touching price. When you must concede, **cut scope, not price**.

---

## The pricing ladder

```
Free value        → builds trust and list
Small paid entry  → audit, teardown, diagnostic  (highest-converting step)
Core offer        → the main product
High ticket       → done-for-you, retainer, intensive
```

Buyers of the small paid entry convert to the core offer at a far higher rate
than cold leads. This step is usually the missing one.
