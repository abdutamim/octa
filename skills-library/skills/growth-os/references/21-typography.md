# 21 — Typography

Typography is the brand's tone of voice. Change nothing but the font and the same
brand reads luxury → youth, formal → fun, adult → kids. Treat type selection as a
positioning decision, not a decoration decision.

Typography ≠ picking a font. It is the art of arranging text so content is clearer,
easier, and better-looking — and every family transmits an emotional message the way
a voice does. The working unit is the letterform: a family is letters engineered to
match each other, which is why mixing families breaks coherence instantly.

---

## The 4 working families

These are the four that carry ~all branding and advertising work. Learn the feeling
each one transmits and assign by feeling, never by taste.

| Family | Feeling transmitted | Use for | Hard limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Serif** (has small strokes/feet at letter ends) | trust, heritage, luxury, formality, history, high quality | print, government / formal brands, banks, consultancies, fashion and perfume, premium anything | — |
| **Sans-serif** (the modern cut: no feet, soft simple ends, no extra detail) | modern, clear, simple, professional, tech | screens, tech companies, apps, any modern brand | — |
| **Display** (built to grab attention; strong visual personality) | attention, distinctiveness | logo wordmarks, main headlines, posters, ads, book-cover titles | NEVER body text — tiring and uncomfortable to read at length |
| **Script** (imitates handwriting / a signature) | elegance, human touch, personal warmth | fashion, cosmetics and beauty, wedding invitations, restaurants and cafés | headlines and accents ONLY, used sparingly — long script text kills readability |

Serif subtypes to know when narrowing: **old-style · modern · slab · transitional**.
Sans-serif also branches (geometric, rounded, …) — every family has sub-personalities;
filter into them instead of scrolling everything.

**The Rolex logic (serif):** a luxury brand picks a classic serif because the serif's
associations — trust, luxury, history, high quality — restate the brand's market
position. The font is the positioning, repeated.

**The Apple logic (sans):** a simple, clean sans that does NOT try to grab attention.
It transmits calm professionalism and stays quiet **so the product is the hero of the
frame**. If the brand's hero is the product, the type must not compete with it.

## Font vs typeface

- **Typeface = the family. Font = one member at one weight.**
- Montserrat is a typeface; Montserrat Light, Medium, and Bold are three fonts of it.
- Practical consequence: one typeface at several weights is a full hierarchy system.
  You do not need a second family to get contrast — you need a second weight.

## Selection process (order is mandatory)

Selection does not start on font sites. It starts in brand strategy — most of the
answer already exists once personality and audience are defined.

1. **Brand personality first.** Ask: luxury or youth? fun/comic or formal? for kids,
   adults, executives, or seniors? Each personality has a native family.
2. **Target audience second.** People do not read type the same way. Kids brand →
   simple, fun letterforms. Law office → trust and professionalism. Do not pass this
   gate until you can name who is reading.
3. **Name the feeling to transmit,** then map it: serif → luxury/trust · sans →
   modern/clear · script → elegance/personal · display → attention.
4. **Maximum 1–2 typefaces per brand.** One Latin + one Arabic at most. The single
   biggest beginner mistake is many fonts — every section of the brand stops looking
   like the others. When pairing scripts, both faces must share the same skeleton and
   spirit (e.g., a geometric Latin sans pairs with Tajawal, which mirrors its bones).

| Brand type | Default family |
|---|---|
| Bank, consultancy, legal | Serif |
| Tech, app, SaaS | Sans-serif |
| Fashion, perfume | Serif (+ script for headlines only) |
| Kids | Simple rounded sans |
| Café, beauty, weddings | Script accents over a plain text face |

## Google Fonts workflow

1. Open **Filters** → categories (serif / sans-serif / display / handwriting) plus the
   **feeling/appearance** filters. Search by the feeling word (calm, futuristic, happy) —
   never by scrolling the whole catalog.
2. Shortlist several candidates. **Download → install → test on the actual logo** before
   committing. Judge the lockup, not the specimen page.
3. **The logo font is display-duty.** A face that is perfect on the mark and headlines
   will exhaust the eye in paragraphs. Always pick a simpler companion face for long
   text and details; the logo face is the primary/logo typeface only.
4. Arabic sourcing: **عرب فونت (arabfonts)** — the dedicated Arabic-font library.
5. Deliver the choice in black and white first (matches the mono-first logo rule in 04).

## Case study — classic-cars brand

Before searching, answer three questions: **what does it sell · who does it address ·
what should people feel when they see it.**

- Chosen direction: **futuristic / wide-extended sans-serif.**
- Why: the brand is classic cars — it must keep the feeling of luxury and value **without
  looking old or traditional**. A futuristic sans supplies the modern half; the width
  supplies the heritage weight.
- **Wide, extended letters = strength and stability.**
- **Minimal detail = stays readable and clear.**
- **Slightly rounded edges = the bridge between modern and classic** — the one feature
  that lets a geometric sans carry a heritage brand.
- A genuinely beautiful serif candidate was tested and **rejected: right look, wrong
  message.** "It looks good" is not a criterion; message-match is the only criterion.
- The logo letterform (the C) was extracted as the brand icon — pick a face whose key
  letter can survive as a standalone mark.
- Approve the final lockup on black AND on white before locking.

Type-description pattern to ship with every selection (write one per brand):

```
[Family + cut]: geometric sans, sharp terminals softened by light rounding.
Transmits: stability and confidence.
Bridges: modern ↔ classic — matches a heritage product sold to a modern buyer.
Duty: logo + headlines only; body text set in [simpler companion face].
```
