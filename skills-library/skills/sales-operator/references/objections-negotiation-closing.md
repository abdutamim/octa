# Objections, Negotiation, And Closing

Use this when a buyer pushes back, asks for better terms, hesitates, or is ready for a next step.

## Objection Diagnosis

Before responding, classify the concern:

| Concern type | What it means | Seller action |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Buyer does not see enough benefit | Reconnect to discovered outcome |
| Budget | Buyer may not be able to pay | Clarify budget reality and options |
| Price comparison | Buyer compares alternatives | Compare on fit, tradeoffs, and total value |
| Timing | Buyer may need later action | Decide nurture vs urgency based on facts |
| Trust | Buyer lacks confidence | Provide verifiable proof only |
| Authority | Buyer cannot decide alone | Map decision process |
| Indecision | Buyer fears making the wrong decision | Reduce risk and simplify the next step |
| Wrong fit | Offer does not match need | Disqualify respectfully |

## Objection Handling Pattern

1. Acknowledge the concern.
2. Clarify the type of concern.
3. Check whether the original need still matters.
4. Offer a truthful response or next diagnostic question.
5. Decide: continue, negotiate, nurture, or disqualify.

Do not argue, corner, guilt, or pressure the buyer.

## Negotiation Prep

Before negotiating, define:

- Desired outcome.
- Walk-away point.
- Buyer interests.
- Seller interests.
- Objective criteria.
- Tradeable items.
- Non-negotiables.
- Approval limits.

Never negotiate from invented authority or hidden constraints.

## Closing

Closing is appropriate only when fit is confirmed.

Checklist:

- Problem and desired outcome are clear.
- Offer fit is real.
- Buyer understands key terms.
- Decision process is clear.
- Any material risk or limitation is disclosed.
- Next step has owner and date.

If the buyer is ready but fit is weak, do not close. Re-qualify or disqualify.
