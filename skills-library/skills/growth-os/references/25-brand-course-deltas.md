## The 3-part brand story framework (Catalyst / Core Truth / Proof)

Not the same file as the 5 story angles in `05-content-system.md` (those are
macro life-narrative angles). This is the **positioning story** — the three
things a brand must be able to answer before it writes a single post.

| Part | Answers | 3 diagnostic questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Catalyst** | Why the brand exists | What needs to change? What do I see that others don't? Why do I feel the need to act on it? |
| **Core truth** | What makes it different | What do I believe that others do not? What about my personality stands out? Why would the audience care — what value does it bring them? |
| **Proof** | How the identity gets reinforced daily | Does every piece of content reinforce the association I want? What case studies establish credibility? If someone hears my name, what's the first thing they think of? |

Do not use the "problem → transformation → outcome" template — it forces a
single dramatic turning point that most brands don't have. Catalyst/Core
Truth/Proof works for brands with no origin-story drama.

## Topic scope: the 80/20 rule + interest stacking

Talk about ONE pillar 80% of the time. The other 20% is **interest
stacking** — your personal, non-pillar interests (hobbies, opinions, taste).

**Why the 20% matters:** it gives the audience more "at-bats" to connect
with you personally, the same way a coworker becomes a friend once you
discover a shared interest beyond the job. 100% pillar is forgettable at
the human level; 100% personality has no authority. Reference case: Joe
Rogan's range (MMA, comedy, hunting, politics) — one shared interest makes
someone a listener, three or four make them a superfan, but only if he
actually talks about all of them on air.

**Expansion rule:** do not add a second pillar topic until (a) you are
already excellent at pillar one, and (b) the audience has given consistent
positive signal on it. When you do expand, expand into an **adjacent**
topic only — a topic with zero credibility bridge to the current one breaks
trust instead of building it.

## Content medium selection (pick before picking a platform)

Choose the medium first, platform second. Four mediums — evaluate each with
its own self-assessment questions before committing:

| Medium | Best native platforms | Ask yourself |
|---|---|---|
| **Written** | LinkedIn, X, email/newsletter | Do you already journal without prompting? Does the idea stand on its own in text with zero spoken explanation — if you'd have to explain it out loud, kill the post. Do you enjoy 10 rounds of editing? |
| **Video** | YouTube, Shorts/Reels/TikTok | Comfortable on camera, or willing to improve in public? Can you handle public feedback on delivery? Can you stay concise ("bunny brain" — knowledgeable people ramble; fix with a stronger editor, not by avoiding video)? |
| **Audio** | Podcast | Confident with zero visual aid (*harder* than video, not easier — words alone must paint the picture)? Do you like 20+ minute deep dives? Workable cadence (monotone is a bit, not a disqualifier)? |
| **Graphic/design** | Carousels, infographics | Curious about Canva/Figma/Adobe? Can you compress a complex idea into one visual? |

**Video is highest-leverage** — one filmed piece yields shorts, podcast
audio, transcript-derived posts, quote graphics, stills. Pick 1 medium to
start; expand once it runs on autopilot.

**Platform philosophy — the "Eye of Sauron" approach:** prioritize 2-3
platforms, never spread evenly. Put ~80% of attention on ONE at a time; the
rest sit in maintenance mode (enough to not decay, no growth push). Rotate
full attention between them; never scale two platforms at once. Still avoid
single-platform risk (cite: a 12-hour US TikTok outage in Feb 2025 wiped
out creators with no other channel).

**Two-lens platform ranking — track both:** absolute leads (raw volume) and
conversion percentage (rate, even on a small audience). A platform winning
on percentage but currently small is where to pour incremental resources —
absolute lead count catches up once you scale a high-converting platform.

## The Accordion Method (volume vs. effort-per-post)

Quality is not a creator's subjective call — **the audience determines
quality**, and volume is the fastest way to collect that data.

```
Phase 1 — EXPAND the accordion: post high volume, low polish, to learn fast
          what the audience actually resonates with. Do not over-produce
          during this phase; you are collecting signal, not making art.
Phase 2 — COMPRESS the accordion: once you know what "quality" means to
          THIS audience, cut volume and put far more effort into fewer,
          higher-value pieces.
```

Worked comparison: 4 videos/month at 250K views each (60-70% re-watched by
the *existing* audience) vs. 1 video/month at 1M views (majority *new*
eyeballs). Same total views, different brand value — new-audience reach
compounds, existing-audience reach does not. Why MrBeast posts every 4-8
weeks instead of weekly: concentrated effort beats steady medium effort.

**Monthly discipline: study your own top 10%.** Every 30-90 days, pull the
top 10% of pieces and find the ONE or TWO things they share (only what
repeats across multiple winners, not everything you can theorize). Re-run
monthly — do not keep optimizing off learnings that are 6 months stale.

## Per-post storytelling structure: Hook → Problem → Journey → Lesson → CTA

Distinct from the 5 macro story angles in `05-content-system.md` — this is
the shape of a single piece of content, not the brand's overall narrative.
Treat it as a loose guide, not doctrine.

| Beat | Function | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | Win attention before they scroll | Short-form: **2-3 seconds**. Long-form (YouTube): **15-20 seconds**, not the "30 seconds" often quoted. Techniques: contrarian statement ("views don't matter"), pattern interrupt (say something unexpected mid-sentence), thought-provoking question. |
| **Problem** | Set stakes | Small and relatable, not maximally dramatic — audience must see themselves in it. |
| **Journey** | How it resolved | Show the messy middle, not just the win — people resonate with doubt and progress more than a clean victory lap. |
| **Lesson** | Make it actionable | A concrete, chunked takeaway — without it, it's entertainment, not value. Tie back to your expertise/offer when honest. |
| **CTA** | Ask for one action | Not always a sale. "Comment your biggest takeaway" is a valid CTA — it converts passive viewing into action. |

**Story source categories** (pick one, don't force all five into a piece):
origin (keep telling it — you're always talking to a cold audience, even
once you're sick of your own story), failure (pair with the lesson, never
a sob story alone), before/after success, client/customer success (proof
the method replicates beyond you), and industry-contrarian (take a trope
everyone repeats and argue the opposite, respectfully).

## 70/20/10 content experimentation split

Applies to an established content operation, not day-one output.

```
70%  proven formats — the reliable bread-and-butter that already converts
20%  iteration — small tweaks to what already works (new hook, new CTA,
     same subject in a new format)
10%  pure experimentation — new formats, new topics, new creative approach
```

Most teams get stuck at 100% in the 70% bucket because it's comfortable.
Comfort does not build a durable brand — keep at least one foot in the 10%
zone every cycle, or the format will plateau when the platform or audience
shifts. Optional operational add-on: a periodic "content hackathon" — a
full day where the team makes off-the-wall, zero-consequence experimental
content, prize for the best swing.

## Monetization mechanics

**Formula: share the knowledge, sell the execution.** Give away the full
method for free — not a watered-down teaser. The value of free content is
what makes the paid execution ("I know what to do, but I don't want to do
it myself / can't do it as well") feel obviously worth paying for. Fear of
"giving away too much" is backwards: more value given away creates more
demand, not less.

**The offer stack — a 5-rung ladder, build one rung at a time:**

| Rung | Price band | Form |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Free content | $0 | builds trust and awareness |
| 2. Lead magnet | $0 (email/phone capture) | mini-course, SOP, checklist, playbook |
| 3. Low ticket | $10–$100 | ebook, mini-course, template |
| 4. Mid ticket | $500–$5,000 | group coaching, community membership, in-depth course |
| 5. High ticket | $10,000+ | 1:1 consulting, agency retainer, done-for-you |

Start with ONE offer, refine it, then add the next rung. Do not launch a
high-ticket offer before trust and awareness exist at scale — the rare
exception is pre-existing demand, which most operators do not have yet.

**Let content do the selling** (5 tactics instead of a hard pitch): teach a
lesson with a soft CTA · share a client success story · document your
process behind the scenes · pre-answer a common objection · use a lead
magnet to capture contact data. Copy swap: not *"Buy my ebook on personal
branding"* but *"I helped John go from 0 to 10K followers in 4.5 months
using this exact strategy — full system is in the ebook, link in bio."*
Results-first framing beats product-first framing.

**Trust chain:** free content → audience trusts your claims → trusts the
lessons enough to act → gets results → audience associates **you with
their own success**, not just yours. That reversal is what makes the
eventual offer feel pre-sold rather than pitched.
