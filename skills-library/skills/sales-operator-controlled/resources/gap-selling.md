## 7. Cross-book Comparison Questions

### SPIN Selling

- Keenan's Current-State elements (Problem → Impact → Root Cause) closely parallel SPIN's Problem → Implication questions. Are "Impact" and "Implication" the same construct under different names, and how do we avoid vocabulary collisions?
- SPIN warns against early Need-payoff; Keenan pushes hard quantification of inaction cost. Where do these reinforce vs conflict?

### Challenger Sale

- Keenan has the buyer surface/quantify their own gap; Challenger has the seller *teach* unrecognized problems via commercial insight. Complementary or competing for the same discovery moment? (Note: Keenan cites Challenger's own 40/7 and 5.4 data.)

### JOLT Effect

- Keenan treats stalls as insufficient gap/urgency; JOLT attributes much late-stage loss to **fear of failure / indecision**, recommending risk reduction rather than more pain. When does widening the gap backfire?

### Influence

- "Next Yes" resembles commitment/consistency; the "I'm confused" challenge touches on consistency pressure. Which Gap-Selling moves are principled persuasion vs the manipulation Keenan claims to reject?

### Getting to Yes

- Gap Selling's "don't discount, defend the gap" stance vs interest-based negotiation: how do quantified gaps interact with BATNA/interests, and where does selling hand off to negotiating?

### Cross-cutting questions

- **Sourcing standard:** several Gap-Selling figures trace to other books/blogs (Challenger, OMG, Gong, CSO Insights). Any cross-book layer should cite originals, not Gap Selling's retelling.
- **Multi-stakeholder reality (5.4 buyers):** all five books assume different buyer structures — reconcile before generalizing.
- **Correlation vs causation:** Gong/CSO/InsideSales figures are correlational; keep that caveat across comparisons.
