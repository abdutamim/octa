# Image Direction — Angle, Gaze, and Argument

> **The image argues the copy. It does not decorate it.**
> If you can swap the image for any other "nice" image and the meaning
> survives unchanged, the image is wrong.

---

## The Test

Ask this before you choose any image:

**"Does this image say the same sentence the headline says?"**

Worked examples across different accounts:

| Headline | Image | The link |
|---|---|---|
| "Cut your ad spend in half" (SaaS) | 3D hand pressing a slider down | the word "spend" sits **directly under the fingers** — the verb is drawn |
| "Set the type big. Small type dies." (any brand) | one tiny figure on an empty salt flat | the image **is the failure state** — the body copy is genuinely unreadable |
| "Your menu hides your best dish" (restaurant) | dense black forest opening into a lit clearing | the move from opaque to clear **is** the meaning |
| "Start at the hardest rep. No warm-up talk." (fitness coach) | a figure with a cardboard box over their head in a field | no context, no face, no explanation — **that is the definition of a cold open** |
| "The clause nobody reads" (law firm) | a suited figure sitting on a cloud above the sea | isolation — the thing you notice alone |
| "Space" (the whitespace rule, ceramicist) | the word itself surrounded by enormous emptiness | **the slide executes its own rule** |
| "Your synth is a collaborator" (music producer) | top-down: person and gear side by side on the same rug | **partner, not tool** — same level, same size |

**The inverse:** a "nice stock photo" behind copy = zero argument. Reference
teardowns call this "generic and overused" and use it as the failure case.

## Camera Angle — a Meaning Decision

| Angle | What it says | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **90° top-down** | flattens the room into a **2D graphic surface** | ← strongest use: white copy sits **on** the image without looking pasted, because no perspective contradicts it |
| **Low angle** | authority, stature, "it looks up at you" | the hero, the brand, the achievement |
| **Eye level** | equality, conversation | explainer slides, the CTA |
| **High 45°** | vulnerability, confession, intimacy | the emotional / admission slide |
| **Very wide + hand forward** | touch, confrontation, **breaking the screen** | a cover that has to stop the thumb by force |
| **Telephoto, subject very small** | isolation, being lost, "you have no map" | "don't know where to start", "no system" |
| **Drone / aerial** | cinematic, distance, reflection | the philosophical or closing line |
| **POV / from the subject's eyes** | "you are in the scene" | personal narrative, diary posts |
| **Flat orthographic (no tilt)** | **evidence** | screenshots, mockups, data. Tilt says "decoration"; flat says "document" |

## Beat Tagging — Mood + Energy + Shot Type per Slide

The chassis (6–8 slides) **is the beat list**. Before you generate any image,
tag every slide:

```text
Slide        Mood          Energy      Shot type
1 cover      tension       high        wide establishing
2 mirror     isolation     low         long shot, subject small
3 objection  quiet defiance medium     close-up
4 mechanism  clarity       building    top-down
5 grid       —             —           designed background (no shot)
6 truth      weight        high        low angle
7 CTA        stillness     zero        no image
```

**Hard rule: never repeat the same shot type twice in a row.**
This turns "vary the treatment each slide" from advice into a checkable
condition.

**And the energy curve:** low → building → high at the reveal → **zero at the
CTA**. The CTA kills energy by subtraction, and that is what brakes the scroll.

---

## Red Line: Likeness

**Generate the likeness of the account owner only, from their own photos.**
Never generate a client's face, a team member's face, or a face for a
testimonial. The red lines in [copy.md](copy.md) cover invented numbers — this
one covers faces.

## When There Is No Person

Most accounts are faceless: product-led, service-led, illustrated, or run by
someone who will not be photographed. Nothing above requires a human. Pick a
recurring **subject** instead and treat it exactly like a person — same angles,
same beat tagging, same one-shoot cohesion.

| Mode | What the subject is | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Object on a surface** | one hero object, one surface, one light | product lines, food, ceramics, tools, packaging |
| **Screen in context** | a real screenshot inside a physical scene (on a desk, held, propped) | SaaS, apps, dashboards, courses |
| **Abstract / typographic** | the words are the image: scale, void, one material | legal, finance, philosophy, anything with no photogenic object |
| **Illustration** | a drawn world with one palette and one line weight | schools, health, sensitive topics, anything explanatory |

The rules that reference "the subject" apply unchanged. The only rules that go
away are the AI-face ones — a faceless account never enters that risk.

## Gaze Economy

```text
Default across all slides  →  face hidden · from behind · profile · eyes averted
```

**Why:**
1. **An anonymous subject lets the reader stand in.** A back or a silhouette
   reads as "that could be me." A clear, front-facing face reads as "that's
   them."
2. **The copy stays the hero.** A face always takes attention; any copy in the
   same frame loses to it.
3. **AI reproduces faces badly** the larger and more frontal they get — see the
   section below.

**And direct-to-camera?** Reference accounts show a pattern of "the face
appears once, at the CTA." That pattern was **tested here and rejected** — do
not apply it by default. If it is explicitly requested: the face gets the slide
alone, **with zero copy over it**.

**Even with the gaze averted, aim the body at the copy.** An outstretched arm,
hair blown one way, the direction of a stride — each one is a visual stake that
pulls the eye to the headline.

### Gaze for non-human subjects

An object has a gaze too. It is **where the object points and where its implied
energy leads**.

- **Direction:** the spout, blade, handle, cable, opening, or long axis is the
  nose of the face. Point it at the headline, never off the frame away from it.
- **Motion:** a pour, a spill, steam, a shadow, a stack about to fall — the
  implied vector should terminate in the copy block, not in empty margin.
- **Facing:** a screen or open page tilted toward the reader is "eye contact";
  tilted away is "profile." Use tilted-away as the default for the same reason
  the averted face is the default — it keeps the copy in front.
- **Isolation:** one small object in a large empty surface is the object version
  of the tiny-figure long shot. Same meaning, no person needed.

## Choosing the Subject

| Case | Choose |
|---|---|
| Explainer slide | from behind · covered face (helmet, box, mask) · eyes closed · silhouette · or the object alone |
| Mirror / pain | subject small in an empty frame — isolation |
| Authority | low angle · arms folded · planted stance · or the object shot from below |
| Confession | high 45° · head tilted · soft light |
| CTA | **no image** by default. If forced: the image *is* the invitation (empty chair, open hand, open door) and the copy sits in void, far from any face |
| Sensitive topic (medical, financial, legal) | **no people.** One hero object on a controlled background |

**Impossible images stop the thumb:** a head buried in sand · an astronaut
reading in a field of flowers · a suited figure on a cloud · a chair set for
one at the bottom of a pool. The strangeness buys the half-second the headline
needs to be read.

## 🚨 AI-Generated Faces — Read This Before You Generate

**Confirmed in real production:** AI **does not reproduce a person's face**. It
returns "someone who looks like them." The person whose face it is spots the
difference instantly — you will not.

Failure scales directly with **how large the face is in the frame and which way
it points**:

| Shot | Fidelity |
|---|---|
| From behind · back to camera | ✅ excellent — no face at all |
| Profile · cropped face · small face in a wide frame | ✅ good |
| Hands · details · environment with no person | ✅ excellent |
| Three-quarter, medium face | ⚠️ starts drifting |
| **Frontal portrait, large face** | ❌ **returns a different person** |

### The rule
```text
AI          →  shots where the face is not visible (back, profile, hands, environment)
Real photo  →  any shot where the face is the subject
```

In production: the wall shot (back), the frames shot (back), the screens shot
(back), and the hands shots — **all worked**. The frontal portraits **all
failed**.

### How to work correctly
```powershell
# Default for any shot with the person in it — forces a turned/obscured face
node scripts/gen.mjs --out=... --ref=ref/pose.jpg,ref/portrait.jpg \
  --preset=face-lock-back --prompt="..."

# If a frontal face is unavoidable: generate 3, pick one; if none is a match, use a real photo
node scripts/gen.mjs --out=... --ref=... --preset=face-lock --n=3 --prompt="..."
```

- **Give it two references** — one for pose, one for feature clarity.
- **Do not describe their features in the prompt.** A description gives the
  model permission to draw a new face "matching the description." Say "the
  person in the attached image" and stop.
- **Look at the output next to the reference, side by side.** If you are in
  doubt, it is not them.

> And above all: **if the face is off, do not patch it with design.** Change the
> shot to one where the face is not visible — cleaner, faster, more honest.

## The Lock String — Six Levers, Written Once

> This is what makes 20 images read as **one shoot** instead of 20 separate
> nice pictures. Hand-writing "the visual world" into every prompt **drifts**.
> A file does not drift.

`vibe.json` lives in the project — `gen.mjs` appends it to every prompt
automatically:

| Lever | What it controls | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **stock** | **The single strongest lock.** A film-stock name forces the same grain and contrast every time | Kodak Portra 400 · CineStill 800T |
| **light** | Direction, hardness, time of day. Changes everything before anything else does | hard amber light from a low north window |
| **grade** | Which colors survive and which recede. **"Colorful" is not a grade** | amber and black only, zero cool tones |
| **lens** | Field of view and compression. Pins the perspective | 50mm, shallow depth of field |
| **camera** | Where the camera stands | static, eye level |
| **composition** | How the frame is built | subject low and to one side, top third empty |

```json
{ "lock": { "stock": "...", "light": "...", "grade": "...",
            "lens": "...", "camera": "...", "composition": "..." },
  "signature": "one warm practical lamp in frame",
  "antiStyle": "no stock-photo gloss, no teal-and-orange, no lens flare" }
```

**One lock per project.** New project = new lock. **Never mix.**

### `signature` and `antiStyle` — cheap, and they matter

- **signature:** one element that recurs in every frame (a lamp, a prop, a
  color). Free continuity.
- **antiStyle:** **name the generic look you are refusing.** The model defaults
  back to stock-photo lighting; naming what you don't want is what stops it.

### The realism swap

If the image is "shot too well" for the story — someone talking about their own
workday is not carrying a cinema camera — **swap the stock for a phone and
leave every other lever untouched**:

```powershell
node scripts/gen.mjs ... --realism      # stock → "iPhone"
```

Same mood, suddenly believable. That is the difference between "a nice AI
render" and "someone actually shot this."
**Keep the cover filmic; give the behind-the-scenes slides the swap.**

### Confirm they came out of the same shoot
```powershell
node scripts/probe.mjs --set assets/gen/*.png
```
It measures exposure, saturation, and hue per image and tells you which one is
outside the set. A single drifting image breaks the carousel, and you will not
catch it by clicking through files one at a time.

## Gemini Prompts

```powershell
node scripts/gen.mjs --out=assets/p01.png --preset=plate --prompt="..." --dry
```

**`--dry` before any batch.** It prints the final prompt without sending. A
wrong lock string × 8 images = money and time.

### The shape
```
[subject + action] · [place] · [angle and crop] · [the empty space you need]
```
**Light, grade, and lens are not written here** — those live in the lock string.
The prompt describes **the scene**; the file describes **the world**.

### Templates

**Plain plate (with room for copy):**
```
A lone figure in a dark coat walking away across a salt flat at dusk,
seen from behind, tiny in the frame. Long lens, eye level, deep focus.
Overcast sky filling the top two thirds, horizon low at 70%.
Cold desaturated palette, fine film grain.
```
`--preset=plate` adds the negative-space request and the no-text guard
automatically.

**Any shot with a real person — the default:**
```powershell
node scripts/gen.mjs --out=... --ref=ref/pose.jpg,ref/portrait.jpg --preset=face-lock-back --prompt="..."
```
`face-lock-back` injects the likeness-protection condition **and** forces a
non-frontal face. It is the default because fidelity collapses on frontal
faces — see the section above. Plain `face-lock` is only for a deliberate
portrait, and always with `--n=3`.

**Prepping for a cutout:**
```powershell
node scripts/gen.mjs --out=... --preset=cutout-ready --prompt="..."
```
Requests a clean silhouette edge and tonal separation, so `cutout.py` comes out
clean.

**Extending a landscape image:**
```powershell
node scripts/gen.mjs --out=... --ref=wide.jpg --preset=outpaint --prompt="extend upward"
```

### 🚫 Don't
- **Don't ask for text inside the image.** Gemini mangles letterforms, and
  non-Latin scripts come out as scribble. Generate a clean image and set the
  typography in code. Every preset blocks text.
- **Don't generate real UI.** It hallucinates interfaces. Take a real
  screenshot.
- **Don't composite a cutout of a person onto another photo of them** → you get
  two of them in the frame.

## One Shoot for the Whole Carousel

The cheapest and strongest source of visual cohesion is **a single controlled
variable set**: hold everything constant except one thing, and vary only that
one thing across the slides.

In an 8-slide teardown, cohesion came **90% from wardrobe and location, and 0%
from filters** — no duotone, no desaturation, no blur on any slide. Same room,
same clothes, same accessories, every frame.

Worked examples of the same principle:

- **A product line on one surface, one light.** A ceramicist shoots eight pieces
  on the same slab of stone under the same window. The pieces change; the
  surface, the light direction, and the camera height do not.
- **An illustrated set with one palette and one line weight.** A language school
  runs eight scenes drawn in three colors with one stroke width. The scenes
  change; the palette and the stroke do not.
- **A location series in one building.** A restaurant shoots eight frames in one
  room across one service — pass, table, door, bar. The vantage changes; the
  room, the hour, and the light do not.

So instead of hunting for 8 images and trying to grade them into resembling
each other:
1. **Shoot 8 frames in one sitting** — or generate them with the same refs and
   the same place.
2. **Change the stance and the prop each frame**, not the place.
3. **The prop is the argument:**

```text
printout held up          → audit and review        (cover: "are you actually checking?")
full order box in hand    → the payoff              (promise slide)
peering over the top of it → "caught you reading"   (hook slide)
mid-sip, cup raised       → the pause, the stop     (mirror slide)
table covered in notes    → the long sit            (content slide)
napkin and pen            → "write this one down"   (grid slide)
leaning back, cup down    → indifference            (hard-truth slide)
no image                  → move now                (CTA)
```

For a faceless account, the same list runs on objects: the printout, the box,
the notes, and the empty surface carry the identical beats with no one in
frame.

Every prop argues its own slide. This is cheaper than 8 separate visual
concepts, and stronger.

## Graphics Over the Image

- **Keep the image alive.** A global darkening kills it. If you need darkening,
  make it local.
- **Grade it into the palette** before you place it — `grade.mjs --duotone` or
  `--preset=filmic`. A raw photo on a brand background always reads as pasted
  on.
- **Push the cohesion further:** put the frame color **inside the scene** — a
  laptop screen, a neon sign, a garment. The strongest teardown did exactly
  this: the frame's terracotta reappearing on the screen in the photo.
- **Pull the copy color out of the image itself.** One reference set the
  headline in the exact green of the plants in the shot — which is why the copy
  reads as welded into the frame rather than sitting on top of it.
