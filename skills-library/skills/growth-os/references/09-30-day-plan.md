# 09 — The 30-Day Plan

This is where advice becomes execution. **"Post valuable content" is banned.**
Every day must carry a concrete deliverable the user can execute without asking
a follow-up question.

---

## Three numbers before writing anything

1. **Time** — hours per week, honestly.
2. **Budget** — zero means a pure organic plan; money means an ads layer.
3. **Goal** — one number (leads, sales, followers, first client).

| Hours/week | Posts/week | Split |
|---|---|---|
| < 3 | 3 | 2 Connect + 1 Consider |
| 3–6 | 5 | 2 Connect + 2 Consider + 1 Close |
| > 6 | 7 | 3 Connect + 3 Consider + 1 Close |

> A small plan that ships beats a big plan that stalls. When unsure, go smaller.
> Volume is the growth lever — but only volume that actually happens.

---

## Week themes

### Week 1 — Foundation & visibility
Goal: a visitor understands who you are and who you serve within 5 seconds.
- Lock the profile: photo, name field, 3-line bio, link, highlights.
- Lock the visual identity (`vibe.json`, palette, type, chassis).
- **3 introduction posts:** who you are · who you help · your contrarian take.
- Build the lead magnet and its keyword.
- Ads (if any): pixel, events, UTM setup only. **No spend yet.**

### Week 2 — Trust & proof
- Consider posts: frameworks, how-tos, behind-the-scenes, teardowns.
- **Anchor post #1** — the strongest piece of the month.
- First proof content: a real result, before/after, or review.
- Activate the lead magnet via DM keyword.
- Ads: small test, 2–3 distinct angles.

### Week 3 — Conversion
- Close posts: the offer stated plainly · biggest objection answered ·
  case study · "who this is NOT for".
- First clear hard sell.
- Landing page or standard DM script finalized.
- **Anchor post #2.**
- Direct outreach: 5–10 genuine messages/day to people who engaged.

### Week 4 — Expand & repeat
- Top 3 posts → 5 new posts each (see `05`).
- Kill what didn't work: format, timing, or angle.
- Written retro + the next 30-day plan.
- Ads: stop losers, scale winners 20–30% every 2–3 days.

## Weekly distribution (5-post cadence)

```
Sat   Connect   reel that breaks an expectation
Mon   Consider  educational carousel (highest saves)
Tue   Connect   personal story or moment
Thu   Consider  teardown of a case or tool
Fri   Close     proof or offer
Daily 2–3 stories + reply to comments within the first 30–60 minutes
```

Stories are the CTA channel: they are seen almost exclusively by followers, which
makes them the best place to ask for a click. **Early on, with few followers,
deprioritize stories** — put 80% of effort into reels, which reach non-followers.

---

## Batch production

```
1 filming day    → 3–5 reels
1 design session → the whole week's carousels in one form
1 writing session→ the whole week's captions
```
**Consistency beats perfection.** A 7/10 post that ships beats a 10/10 post that
never does. Batching is what makes high volume physically possible.

---

## Weekly retro (30 minutes)

```
1. Top 3 posts — and why (hook? format? topic?)
2. Bottom 3 — and why
3. How many leads / DMs / customers?
4. What do we repeat, what do we kill?
5. ONE change for next week
```
**Change one variable at a time**, or you will never know what caused the result.

## Metrics by stage

| Stage | Metric |
|---|---|
| Connect | Reach · % non-followers · average watch time |
| Consider | Saves · shares · profile visits |
| Close | DMs · link clicks · leads · sales |

Likes are not a metric. **Saves, shares, and DMs tell the truth.**

---

## Realistic expectations (say this out loud)

- Month 1 is **foundation and learning**, not an explosion.
- Average organic growth for a small business account is roughly **8% per month**.
  Viral stories are the exception you hear about, not the base rate.
- Commit for **at least 6 months**, realistically a year, before judging whether
  the approach works.
- A new account reaching 3–10k on a good post is normal — and often **more
  useful** than a million views from the wrong audience.
- **Never compare outcomes to established creators.** Comparing growth rates to
  people who started years earlier is the single fastest way to quit.
- Promise no numbers. Give ranges and state the assumptions.
