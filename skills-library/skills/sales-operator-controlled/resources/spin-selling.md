## 6. Cross-book Comparison Questions

### Challenger Sale

- SPIN has the buyer surface needs through questioning; Challenger has the seller *teach* the buyer about unrecognized problems with commercial insight. Where do "Implication questions" end and "teaching/reframing" begin — complementary or contradictory?
- Does Challenger's "take control / push" conflict with SPIN's caution about buyer pressure?

### Gap Selling

- How does SPIN's Implied→Explicit progression map onto Gap Selling's explicit current-state vs future-state gap (technical, business, *and* emotional dimensions)?
- Is the "value equation" the same construct as Gap Selling's quantified gap?

### JOLT Effect

- SPIN treats stalled deals largely as insufficient need/value development. JOLT attributes indecision to buyer *fear of failure*. When is more Implication questioning counter-productive (could it deepen overwhelm), and when is risk-mitigation the better move?

### Influence

- SPIN warns against pressure tactics; Influence catalogs persuasion levers (reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, scarcity, liking). Which levers are compatible with SPIN's diagnostic stance, and which read as the "closing pressure" SPIN cautions against?
- Is SPIN's Need-payoff (buyer voices the benefit) a form of the commitment/consistency principle?

### Getting to Yes

- SPIN is buyer-needs discovery within a sale; GTY is interest-based negotiation. How do "Explicit Needs" relate to "interests vs positions"? Where does discovery hand off to negotiation, and do the two stages need different behaviors?

### Cross-cutting questions

- **Multi-stakeholder reality:** SPIN transcripts are largely 1:1; how does it scale to a buying committee?
- **Asynchronous / digital & PLG motions:** SPIN assumes a live call and a discovery→presentation split; how do the behaviors translate when buyers self-serve or evaluate via shared docs before talking to a rep?
- **Causation vs correlation:** every comparison should keep Appendix A's caution in view — SPIN's evidence is largely observational/correlational.
