## 9. Cross-book Comparison Questions

### SPIN Selling

- How does **Hypothesis-Based Selling** (lead with an educated guess of the pain) reconcile with SPIN's discipline of letting the customer surface pain via Situation/Problem/Implication questions? (Note: Rackham wrote Challenger's foreword.)

### Gap Selling

- Is the **Challenger Reframe** the same mechanism as Gap Selling's Current→Future gap expansion, or does Challenger add *outside-in* commercial insight the buyer couldn't reach alone?
- Both cite the 53% sales-experience-loyalty figure and the 5.4-buyer consensus reality — confirm both trace to the *same* CEB/Challenger research and avoid double-counting.

### JOLT Effect

- Challenger uses **constructive tension** to push the customer; JOLT argues late-stage loss is often **fear/indecision**, requiring de-risking rather than more pressure. When does adding tension backfire?

### Influence

- How do **Teach/Reframe** and the 6-step choreography map to Authority, Liking, and Commitment/Consistency — and where does "constructive tension" risk crossing into manipulation?

### Getting to Yes

- How does **DuPont SSN** (Acknowledge & Defer, Deepen & Broaden, Explore & Compare, Concede According to Plan) align with principled negotiation (interests over positions, options for mutual gain, BATNA)?

### Cross-cutting questions

- **Single-source caution:** most Challenger statistics come from one organization's (CEB) proprietary datasets; any cross-book layer should note that and seek independent replication.
- **Complexity boundary:** all five books assume different deal complexity — fix the boundary conditions before generalizing.
