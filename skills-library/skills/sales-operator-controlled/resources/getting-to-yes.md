## 2. Core Thesis

*Getting to Yes* argues for principled negotiation: negotiate on the merits rather than through positional bargaining, pressure, tricks, posturing, or a contest of will.

The central posture is "hard on the merits, soft on the people." The core method is organized around people, interests, options, and criteria: separate the people from the problem; focus on interests, not positions; invent options for mutual gain; and insist on objective criteria.

BATNA is the no-agreement alternative used as self-protection. A party should compare a proposed agreement with what it will actually do if no agreement is reached. A trip wire can warn before drifting below an acceptable range.

Objective criteria and fair procedures are positioned as protection against pressure: parties should reason from legitimate standards rather than yield to threats, will, arbitrary demands, or fake legitimacy.

The passes preserve a major ethical point: no agreement is an acceptable outcome when a party's BATNA is better than the proposed agreement. The method is practical advice, not a moral sermon. The authors explicitly frame the book as helping a negotiator do well while protecting decency, durability, relationship quality, and process quality.

## 3. Extracted Principle Clusters

### 1. Positional Bargaining Problem

- Definition: Sequential take-a-position-then-concede bargaining, in hard or soft form.
- Main argument: Positional bargaining tends to produce unwise agreements, inefficiency, damaged relationships, and worse outcomes when multiple parties are involved.
- Key concepts: hard bargaining, soft bargaining, saving face, contest of will, extreme openings, small concessions, bottom-line concealment, deception, foot-dragging, relationship damage.
- Book support: Pass 1 extracts Chapter 1's critique and the three method criteria: wise agreement, efficiency, and relationship preservation.
- Evidence type: Direct author framework; historical illustration; typified dialogue.
- Ethical implications: Flags pressure, deception, will contests, and relationship damage as costs to avoid.
- Buyer-autonomy relevance: High; positional pressure risks overriding independent judgment.
- Sales-skill relevance candidate: CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Useful as a warning against haggling-style sales pressure.
- Manipulation / misuse risk: High if used to justify counter-pressure or clever bargaining moves.
- Guardrails needed: Do not adopt hard bargaining, extreme anchoring, concealment, or pressure tactics.
- Verification status: Core author framework; illustrative cases are not proof; universal performance claims remain unverified.
- Approval status: CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED.

### 2. Principled Negotiation

- Definition: Negotiation on the merits using people, interests, options, and criteria.
- Main argument: There is a third way beyond hard or soft bargaining: decide issues by merits, search for mutual gain, and use fair standards where interests conflict.
- Key concepts: people / interests / options / criteria; wise agreement; no tricks; no posturing; analysis / planning / discussion; hard on merits, soft on people.
- Book support: Pass 1 extracts the introduction and Chapter 1 framing; Pass 4 confirms the Analytical Table of Contents matches this structure.
- Evidence type: Direct author framework.
- Ethical implications: Strong anti-pressure and anti-trick stance, while remaining practical rather than moralistic.

- Sales-skill relevance candidate: CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Active listening, perception-checking, and relationship/substance separation are candidate concepts only.

- Book support: Pass 2 extracts Chapter 4 in full; Pass 4 confirms the Analytical TOC includes the Circle Chart.
- Evidence type: Direct author framework; historical illustration; illustrative case; practitioner guidance.
- Ethical implications: Strong mutual-gain orientation, but some legitimacy / consistency framing is dual-use.
- Buyer-autonomy relevance: High when options preserve choice; open tension with option overload remains unresolved.
- Sales-skill relevance candidate: CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Candidate concept for collaborative option design only.
- Manipulation / misuse risk: Medium; "make their decision easy" can become steering or narrowing without consent.
- Guardrails needed: No hidden narrowing; no legitimacy engineering; no using options to overwhelm or channel a buyer.

### 7. BATNA and Trip Wire

- Definition: BATNA is the Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement; trip wire is a package above BATNA that triggers pause and reassessment.
- Main argument: Power depends mainly on the attractiveness of each side's no-deal option; BATNA is the true standard for judging offers and protects against bad agreements.
- Key concepts: bottom line critique; no-deal alternative; invent / improve / select alternatives; reveal strong BATNA, conceal weak BATNA; consider their BATNA; trip wire; no agreement as acceptable.
- Book support: Pass 3 extracts Chapter 6 in full; Pass 4 expands power and micro-BATNA in Q10.

### 8. Negotiation Jujitsu

- Definition: A response to positional bargaining that avoids pushing back and redirects attacks toward the problem.
- Main argument: Do not react to positional moves with counterattack; look behind positions, invite criticism, recast attacks on the person as attacks on the problem, ask questions, and pause.
- Key concepts: do not push back; look behind their position; invite criticism and advice; questions instead of statements; silence; redirect force to the problem.
- Book support: Pass 3 extracts Chapter 7 in full.
- Evidence type: Direct author framework; historical illustration; typified dialogue.
- Ethical implications: Often autonomy-respecting because it avoids counterattack, but several sub-moves are subtle influence mechanics.
- Buyer-autonomy relevance: Medium to high; can preserve face and participation, but can also become pressure if scripted.
- Sales-skill relevance candidate: CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Open question only; not a script source.
- Manipulation / misuse risk: High for strategic silence, flattering assumptions, praise that creates emotional investment, or stock phrases.
- Guardrails needed: No jujitsu lines as scripts; no strategic silence as pressure; no emotional investment engineering.
- Verification status: Direct author framework; examples illustrative; effectiveness claims unverified.
- Approval status: CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED.

### 9. One-Text Procedure

- Definition: A third party or participant drafts one evolving text, invites criticism, revises repeatedly, and eventually recommends a yes/no decision.
- Main argument: A single evolving draft separates inventing from deciding, reduces lock-in, and simplifies decisions.
- Key concepts: draft / criticize / redraft; no one committed to the text; mediator or self-interested participant; final recommendation; yes/no decision; many-to-one decision simplification.
- Book support: Pass 3 extracts the one-text procedure; Pass 4 confirms one-text / Camp David provenance in A Note on the Harvard Negotiation Project and note [3] for South African one-text facilitators.
- Evidence type: Direct author framework; endnote-supported author framework; historical illustration.
- Ethical implications: Autonomy-protective if it reduces premature commitment and keeps criticism open.
- Buyer-autonomy relevance: High if the buyer remains free to criticize and say no.
- Sales-skill relevance candidate: CANDIDATE ONLY — NOT APPROVED. Open question only; not a sales process module.
- Manipulation / misuse risk: Medium if a seller drafts the "one text" to control the frame.
- Guardrails needed: No seller-controlled draft as hidden pressure; criticism must be genuine; no implied commitment.
- Verification status: Provenance corroborated by Pass 4; historical detail remains author-asserted.

## 6. Ethics and Buyer Autonomy Map

- Buyer autonomy: Central candidate value. BATNA, objective criteria, competing standards, face-saving, anti-stereotype caution, and no-deal acceptability all protect independent judgment.
- Mutual gain: Candidate concept only; must be genuine and must not paper over real conflicts.
- Objective criteria: Candidate safeguard against pressure; criteria must be independent, legitimate, practical, and reciprocally applicable.
- Fair standards: Candidate tool for resisting arbitrary will; must not become fake authority.
- Fair procedures: Candidate tool when standards conflict; procedures must be mutually acceptable and not gamed.
- BATNA as self-protection: Candidate ethical guardrail; a party's no-deal alternative protects it from bad agreements.
- Trip wire as self-protection: Candidate early-warning mechanism only; not a pressure tool.
- Dirty tricks as detection only: Recognition and resistance only; never tactics.
- No threats: Threats remain prohibited.
- No pressure: Do not convert negotiation concepts into pressure, urgency, or coercion.
- No deception: Less-than-full disclosure is not a license to mislead.
- No fake legitimacy: Objective criteria must not be fabricated or selectively engineered.
- No fabricated criteria: Market value, precedent, expert opinion, or standards must be real and relevant.
- No buyer-BATNA weaponization: BATNA reasoning must not be used to corner buyers.
- No scripts from illustrative dialogues: Jones Realty / Turnbull and jujitsu examples are not reusable lines.
- No conversion of pressure tactics into sales tactics: Dirty tricks, anchoring, fading opportunities, lock-ins, threats, and strategic silence remain quarantined.
