This document creates no restricted outputs, no scoring tool, no disqualification scorecard, no fixed production labels, and approves nothing.

## 1. Purpose

This is an **internal diagnostic reasoning map** — a thinking aid for identifying which decision state a buyer is most likely in *before* any sales reasoning path is chosen.

It is **not** a sales script, **not** a scoring tool, **not** a disqualification scorecard, and **not** a production framework. The twelve states below are provisional reasoning lenses, not fixed buyer labels: real buyers occupy several states at once, move between states within a single conversation, and can be misread. A state hypothesis is something to hold lightly and revise, never a verdict to act on mechanically. Nothing here tells anyone what to say to a buyer; it tells the internal reasoner what to understand, verify, and avoid.

## 2. Diagnostic Principle

> **"Do not choose a sales move before diagnosing the buyer's state."**

This is the operational form of the first candidate principle (HAD-002: diagnosis before persuasion). The reason it must come first is that **most sales reasoning paths are state-conditional: the right move in one state is the harmful move in another.** Misreading state doesn't just waste effort — it routes the reasoner into exactly the behavior the governance blocks:

- **Unclear problem treated as indecision** → the reasoner reaches for JOLT-style recommendation and narrowing when the problem itself was never established — steering without diagnosis, which violates buyer-owned discovery.
- **Decision fear treated as lack of pain** → the reasoner develops more impact and implication on an already-convinced, anxious buyer — pain amplification on a fearful buyer, which the JOLT layer indicates correlates with *worse* outcomes and which fails Ethics Screen Gate 5.
- **Negotiation issue treated as objection** → a legitimate interests-and-criteria conflict gets "handled" instead of negotiated on the merits — positional pressure replacing fair process.
- **Pressure environment treated as buyer resistance** → a buyer who is being squeezed (by their own organization, by a competitor's tactics, by time) reads as "difficult," and the reasoner adds force where the buyer needed protection and re-centering on criteria.
- **Poor fit treated as a closing challenge** → a deal that should honestly end becomes a conversion project — the exact failure mode the no-deal-legitimacy principle exists to prevent.

Every one of these misreads converts an ethical reasoning tool into a pressure tool *through diagnosis failure alone*, without anyone intending manipulation. That is why state diagnosis precedes everything.

## 4. State-to-Source Routing

The routing logic, stated compactly:

- **SPIN / Gap** own the **problem side of the map (states 1–3)**: establishing whether a problem exists, what it costs, what causes it, what the current state actually is, what a specific future state would be, and when an implied need has become an explicit one. They are the default starting lens, because every other path assumes their work is done.
- **Challenger** is a **narrow supplement to state 1 only**: when the buyer may genuinely not see a problem that evidence suggests is real, bounded teaching/reframing can surface it as a testable hypothesis. It never replaces discovery (states 2–3 still belong to SPIN/Gap), and its tension component remains unapproved.
- **JOLT** owns the **fear side of the map (states 4–5)**: buyers who have accepted the value case but stall from decision fear or option overload. The JOLT boundary is its entry condition — it applies *after* value is understood, never as a substitute for establishing it.
- **Influence** routes to **no state as a tactics source.** It is the detection lens across all twelve states — most intensively in states 9 and 10 — answering one question: are the cues operating in this deal genuine or fabricated, and is anyone (including our side) exploiting shortcuts instead of evidence?
- **Getting to Yes** owns the **negotiation and ethics side (states 6, 7, 11)** and supplies the boundary layer everywhere else: interests behind positions, objective criteria, fair procedure, BATNA as self-protection, no-deal legitimacy, and anti-pressure re-centering (state 10, jointly with Influence, defensively).
- **State 8** has **no fully adequate owner** — Challenger and GTY contribute, but Source-Gap Map v2 flags buying-committee dynamics as a coverage gap. Reasoning there stays provisional until a covering source is approved.
- **State 12** draws on **SPIN, Gap, Influence, and JOLT jointly**, and is the map's most gate-adjacent state: the concepts are sound, but commitment mechanics are exactly where ethical selling degrades into commitment traps, so the Influence guardrails are load-bearing.

## 5. Misdiagnosis Risks

The dangerous misreads, each with its risk, the boundary it stresses, and the safer internal redirection:

| Dangerous misread | Why it is risky | Boundary violated / stressed | Safer internal redirection |
| ----------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Using more pain when the real issue is decision fear (state 4 read as state 2) | Amplifies anxiety in an already-convinced buyer; JOLT-layer evidence (proprietary, correlational) associates it with materially worse outcomes — it is both unethical and ineffective | Ethics Screen Gate 5 (fear exploitation); HAD-002 principle 7; Synthesis §5 pain-expansion tension | Re-diagnose: has the buyer accepted the value case? If yes, switch entirely to risk-reduction reasoning and criteria support |
| Using urgency when the buyer needs risk reduction | Urgency compresses deliberation in exactly the buyer who needs more decision safety; fabricated urgency is blocked outright | HAD-001 (fake urgency/scarcity); Gates 3–5; JOLT/Influence/GTY triple warning (Synthesis §5) | Identify what risk the buyer actually fears; reason about reducing *that* — real constraints may be disclosed as verifiable fact only |
| Using authority/social proof when the buyer needs objective criteria (state 9 or 6 read as a persuasion deficit) | Stacking cues on a verification-seeking buyer deepens distrust and slides toward authority-as-pressure; proof replaces fit analysis | HAD-001 (fake authority/social proof); Gate 11; Influence authority guardrails | Route to contestable evidence: independent standards, checkable claims, invited challenge — things the buyer can verify without trusting anyone |
| Treating a legitimate no-deal as an objection (state 11 read as a closing challenge) | Converts honest misfit into a conversion project; every move thereafter is structurally pressure, because the buyer's interest is no longer the objective | Gate 10 (no-deal legitimacy); HAD-002 principle 9; GTY no-agreement ethics | Run the fit assessment honestly first; if the buyer's alternative is better on their criteria, the correct internal output is acknowledging it |
| Treating buyer caution as resistance | "Resistance" framing invites overcoming behavior — repetition, escalation, pressure — against a buyer doing legitimate diligence | Gate 4 (no pressure); GTY anti-pressure stance; SPIN closing-pressure findings | Reclassify: caution is information. Diagnose what the caution is about (fear? trust? criteria? fit?) and route to that state's path |
| Treating a price position as only a price objection (state 7 read shallowly) | Triggers rebut-or-discount reflexes; skips the interests and standards underneath, where the real resolution lives; invites quarantined moves (anchoring, concession games) | GTY interests-not-positions; Gate 11; quarantine on anchoring/positional tactics | Map the interests behind the position first; then reason from genuine, reciprocal criteria — never from will or concession mechanics |
| Treating stakeholder friction as individual hesitation (state 8 read as state 4) | Pushes the contact to act on fear they don't have, while ignoring the actual blocker — the organization; burns the champion's standing | Synthesis §6 row 10 (single-champion assumption); Source-Gap Map gap flag | Shift the unit of analysis from person to buying group: map stakeholders' interests, criteria, and risks — flag conclusions as provisional given the coverage gap |
| Treating overload as a chance to steer instead of simplify (state 5 exploited) | Overloaded buyers default to shortcut cues — exploiting that is the textbook Influence misuse and is blocked by name | HAD-001 (overload exploitation); Gate 6; JOLT narrowing-vs-GTY-agency tension | Simplify honestly: reduce cognitive burden, keep real options and tradeoffs visible, narrow only transparently and with consent |

## 6. Diagnostic Workflow

1. **Identify what is known vs. assumed.** List the buyer facts actually in evidence; flag everything else as assumption. Diagnosis from assumptions is the root misread.
2. **Identify buyer state.** Form a state hypothesis from the map (§3) — held provisionally, allowing for mixed and shifting states.
3. **Check whether problem, impact, interests, and decision risk are clear.** These four clarity checks discriminate between the states that look alike (2 vs. 4; 5 vs. 4; 7 vs. 6; 8 vs. 4).
4. **Route to the relevant source logic** (§4), respecting each source's entry condition — especially JOLT's (value already understood) and Challenger's (hypothesis, not assertion).
5. **Run the Internal Ethics Screen** on whatever reasoning path was selected — all 12 gates, with the §5 misread table as a targeted re-check.
6. **Decide the work's category:** allowed for internal reasoning / needs explicit approval / defensive-only / blocked / needs verification / needs source coverage (Ethics Screen §6, lowest category wins).
7. **Keep output internal** — marked `INTERNAL CONTROLLED DEVELOPMENT — NOT PRODUCTION APPROVED` — unless and until future explicit approval allows implementation.

## 7. Relationship to Ethics Screen

This map and the Ethics Screen are sequential, not parallel: **the map chooses the reasoning path; the screen decides whether that path may proceed.** Every buyer-state diagnosis — and every piece of development work that follows from one — must pass through `Sales-Operator_Internal-Ethics-Screen-v0.md` before continuing. The dependency runs in both directions: a state diagnosis can route work toward a gated or blocked concept (state 5 → recommendation/narrowing, state 12 → commitment concepts, state 10 → the defensive-only layer), and only the screen's gates and decision categories determine what happens then. Where this map and the screen ever appear to disagree, the screen wins, and beneath both, HAD-001 wins *(Original extension)*.

Two states carry standing screen-flags by design: **state 10 work is always defensive-only**, and **state 11 conclusions must never be softened into retention-of-pursuit** — if the screen's Gate 10 check fails on a state-11 deal, the work is blocked, not redirected.
